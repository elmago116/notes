ABSTRACT
This article presents a personalized recommendation approach of
textual and multimedia resources related to artistic and cultural
points of interest (POIs). This approach exploits linked open data
to retrieve content related to POIs and social media to personalize
their recommendation to the target user. The similarity evalua-
tion between the social user profile and the related material occurs
based on the classic doc2vec model. A preliminary comparative
analysis conducted on 20 real users showed encouraging experi-
mental results in terms of perceived accuracy and beyond-accuracy
metrics.

increasingly present in our lives and cities [ 6]. For example, our

moments of our day. Among the most widespread applications,
there are undoubtedly those that may suggest which resources be-
longing to the cultural heritage (CH) may be of interest to us based
on our preferences and the current physical (e.g., location, weather,
day of the week, means of transport) and social (e.g., social graph)
context [23, 25]. In our previous work [22], we have introduced an
approach that leverages linked open data (LOD) [7] to recommend
textual and multimedia material related to CH points of interest

2 RELATED WORK
The research literature is rich in contributions introducing recom-
mender systems (RSs) of multimedia content [ 8], as well as RSs
of cultural heritage points of interest [20] and itineraries among
them [5, 12]. However, few CH recommenders also suggest textual
and multimedia material semantically correlated with the suggested
cultural and artistic resources. Even fewer are the systems that
suggest it in a personalized way. For example, in [1], the authors
propose a recommender that exploits semantic technologies [14]
and personalization methodologies [13] to assist the user in the ex-
ploration of multimedia collections of museums. In [2], the authors
describe a cultural recommender that takes into account not only
the user’s location but also her interests and preferences. In this
way, the system is able to extend the user experience in exploring
the artistic and cultural heritage. In [11], the authors propose a mo-
bile application able to support users during their visit with digital
storytelling content. This system is, therefore, not a recommender,
nor it works in a personalized way, but it shares with our work the
attempt to enrich the visiting experience with multimedia content
related to the visited resources. In [4], the authors present a chatbot
that accompanies the user during her visit and provides her with
services, information, and narrative content based on her profile
and context. Although the previous systems are interesting and
certainly deserve to be mentioned, they do not take advantage of
linked open data to retrieve material semantically correlated with
the places visited and do not exploit social media to infer the user
profile in order to personalize the visiting experience.
3 PROPOSED SYSTEM
The idea behind the proposed approach is to re-rank the textual and
multimedia resources related to the POIs identified through linked
open data. Specifically, the system introduced in [22] recommended
POIs belonging to the artistic and cultural heritage and supple-
mented this recommendation with textual and multimedia material
related to the POIs. Such content was simply retrieved based on
existing links in the LOD cloud1 between the POIs and resources
such as books, movies, and music artists/songs. The ultimate goal is
to enrich the user experience with supplementary material related
to the POI to visit. This material was proposed without, however,
taking into account the real interests of the active user. For example,
if Alice loves novel books, comedy movies, and pop music, and John
is - differently - more interested in adventure books, science fiction
movies, and rock music, and both of them visit the same POI, the
1https://lod-cloud.net/
previous system suggested to both of them the same textual and
multimedia material.
Differently, the goal of the system proposed herein is to cus-
tomize not only the (already provided) POIs recommendation but
also the material related to them. In particular, the idea is to infer
the user’s interests starting from the content generated by her on
social media (e.g., Facebook2). After that, the POI-related material
based on the semantic links present in the LOD can be reranked
based on the user profile. To perform re-ranking, it is necessary to
identify an appropriate function that allows the system to assess
the similarity degree among the user’s preferences on social media
expressed, for example, by giving a like on a particular resource
(e.g., a movie) and the resources related to the POI in the LOD cloud.
Among the approaches that can be undertaken in defining a
re-ranking algorithm, we have chosen the comparison between vec-
tors. In this case, the candidates to be sorted are nodes of the LOD
graph, each of them representing a textual or multimedia resource
that is always associated with a short text (i.e., an abstract), which
describes its main characteristics. In the case of movies, for exam-
ple, the abstract summarizes in an unstructured way information
such as genre, year, series, director, and synopsis. Starting from an
element in Wikidata3 (represented through an ID), it is possible to
go back to the associated Wikipedia page and from this to obtain
the abstract of the entity. Despite the absence of a rigid structure,
Wikipedia abstracts - given their relevance in the page - often have
an almost identical logical structure between pages of the same
type, thanks also to the use of community standards, and often con-
tain keywords. Taking two movies as an example, both abstracts
could start with “<film title> is a <film type> of the <year> directed
by the <director>”, if the genre is the same (e.g., thriller), this word
will be present in both abstracts in more or less the same position.
Therefore, we assumed that the abstract could be used to represent
semi-structured information for each type of media. For this reason,
we used doc2vec [16] (an extension of the word2vec algorithm [17]
that relies on neural networks to represent entities in a vector space)
to numerically represent elements by transforming their abstracts
into vectors. The use of a doc2vec model makes sense strictly to
a single type of resource, therefore, we have trained three differ-
ent models for the three different types of resource in the system,
namely books, movies, and music artists/songs. To carry out the ex-
perimental tests described in the next section, we trained the three
doc2vec models on the Wikipedia abstracts of about 15,000 books,
32,000 movies, and 18,000 music artists/songs, choosing the English
language. We also chose to generate 100 features for each vector.
Overall, the customization module of the textual and multimedia
material related to the POI is composed of three sub-modules:
(1) The user model creation;
(2) The identification of the resources related to the CH POIs
for the active user;
(3) The presentation of the results and evaluation of the system.

After retrieving the media, the system performs the re-ranking
of the results to be proposed to the active user. The comparison
through cosine similarity is the basis of the calculation of the vector
scores. On this basis, the system performs the re-ranking process
through two different approaches, namelysum and clustering. In the
first approach, the user model is collapsed into a single sum vector
coming from the set of vectors retrieved from social networks and
cold-start forms, such as the one shown in Figure 2, used when the
so-called cold start problem [19] occurs, that is, the social profile
retrieved for the target user is not sufficiently informative. This
is, then, compared with the individual vectors of the “candidate”
media, and a single similarity score is calculated for each media.
Those values (between -1 and 1) are then normalized between 0
and 1 and presented to the user as a percentage.
The second approach arises from considering that any non-
heuristic combination of the user profile vectors could lead to a loss
of information (for example, the average between the vectors of

algorithm by choosing the cosine distance as metric. In this re-

defines the maximum distance within which two points (vectors)

testing various epsilon values, we have in any case noticed the con-
stant formation of a cluster with several elements higher than the
others, which often consists of a single element since we have set
the hyper-parameter min_sample = 1. To obtain higher efficiency
of clustering, a more in-depth analysis should be carried out as
these parameters vary. For the recommender, once the re-ranking
for each media type has been calculated, the system displays the
results view, which includes an explanation of the semantic links
followed in the media retrieval.
A third method, nameddegree, is also included in the results table,
which does not take into account the user model and, therefore,
offers a non-personalized ranking. This is determined simply by
counting the number of semantic links of the Wikidata resources
(i.e., a piece of information retrieved along with the resources), and
ordering the resources by inserting those with a higher number
of links at the top-ranking and then gradually all the others, in
decreasing order. The reason why this method was included is to
supply the tester with a comparison between personalized and non-
personalized recommendation methods. In presenting the results
to the user, we then decided to show only the first 30 resources,
therefore according to a top-k recommendation scheme, with k =
30. By clicking on each of the resources in the list, the user can read

4 EXPERIMENTAL EVALUATION
The experimental test of the system involved 20 participants, in-
cluding four women, ten students, seven office workers, two self-
employed, and one unemployed. The average age of the participants
was 28 years old. Our initial idea was to carry out experimental
trials on a much larger number of users, but the limitations placed
by Facebook hindered our original plan to provide testers with
online access to the system, by forcing us to proceed by inviting
testers one by one. Furthermore, the limitations imposed by the
COVID-19 pandemic made data collection even more complex.
System performance evaluation was performed as follows. For
each non-empty media description, the user was asked to sort the
ranking methods used from best to worst, by dragging and drop-
ping the corresponding label. In this way, it was possible to assign
a score to each method, first of all, to understand the effectiveness
of a personalized recommendation method in such a context, and
secondly, to identify the best method among those proposed. In
the same view, the user was asked to express her degree of agree-
ment/disagreement with the four questions reported in Figure 3,
on a scale of values from 1 to 5 (five-point Likert scale). The so-

5 CONCLUDING REMARKS AND FUTURE
WORKS
In this article, we have proposed an approach to provide the target
user in a personalized way with textual and multimedia material
related to the CH POIs to visit. An experimental evaluation con-
ducted on a small number of real users showed that, in general,
users seem to prefer the personalized approaches to the one based
simply on the semantic connection between multimedia resources
and POIs.
This research work is still in an initial state, so there exist several
limitations that we intend to solve shortly. First of all, we need to
carry out an experimental evaluation involving a higher number of
participants. We had planned a much larger test analysis, but then
the limitations imposed by Facebook and especially those due to the

current COVID-19 pandemic hindered our plans. Another limitation
of our work that we hope to solve in the next future concerns the
comparative analysis. In the experimental evaluation seen above, we
compared the two variants of our approach with a simple baseline
where the multimedia and textual materials related to the POIs are
ranked based on the number of existing semantic links. To the best
of our knowledge, there are no approaches in the literature that
share our same goal, namely, the personalized recommendation of
multimedia and textual content associated with the POIs to visit. For
this reason, we have not compared the performance of our system
with that of other works, except the one based on semantic links.
We, therefore, count on designing and implementing new variants
of the proposed approach to carry out a more comprehensive and
meaningful comparative analysis. We also intend to further increase
the performance of our recommender system in terms of perceived
accuracy and beyond-accuracy metrics. For this purpose, we plan
to also take into account the affective state [18] of the target user,
as well as the temporal evolution [ 3] and real stance [ 10] of her
interests. A final aspect that we would like to develop concerns
the typical use scenario of the system. It often happens that the
visitor is not alone but in a company. We would, therefore, like to
extend our approach by creating a recommender system capable
of meeting the information needs not only of the individual user
but of the entire group of visitors wishing to visit the cultural and
artistic resources of the surrounding area.
