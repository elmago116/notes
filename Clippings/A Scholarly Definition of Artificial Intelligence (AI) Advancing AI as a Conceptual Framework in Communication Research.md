## ABSTRACT

Research on Artificial Intelligence (AI) in communication research is gaining broader interest. This interdisciplinary interest has yet to be supported by a systematic scholarly definition and by a holistic theoretical framework in communication research. First, combining prior theoretical efforts from diverse disciplines in the social sciences, especially journalism and communication, this study introduces a wide-ranging working AI scholarly definition in communication research as *the tangible real-world capability of non-human machines or artificial entities to perform, task solve, communicate, interact, and act logically as it occurs with biological humans*. We also propose its theoretical operationalization based on two dimensions: level of *performance* and level of *autonomy*, advancing an elementary conceptual framework drawing on AI’s levels of potential actions or performance the AI may accomplish, including 1) performing tasks, 2) taking decisions, and 3) making predictions; as well as AI’s level of autonomy, or the agency results contingent on the degrees of human input, interaction, or supervision involved.

Present-day society is saturated by digital infrastructures, supported by artificial technologies, which have become essential in navigating the social world (de Lima-Santos & Ceron, [Citation 2021](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#); Hepp, [Citation 2020](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#); Lobera et al., [Citation 2020](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)). Rapid advances in Artificial Intelligence (AI) have significantly disrupted the world economy and other sectors like engineering, agriculture, politics, and media, developing technological-driven systems in the process of information and service creation, dissemination, and preservation (Birtchnell, [Citation 2018](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#); Kamble & Shah, [Citation 2018](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#); Kieslich et al., [Citation 2022](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#); Tubaro et al., [Citation 2020](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)). The penetration, use, and reliance on AI have various implications for different disciplines, including communication.

Some studies in communication and journalism have problematized the potential impacts of AI in shaping users’ attitudes, perceptions, and behaviors (Brewer et al., [Citation 2022](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#); Chen & Wen, [Citation 2021](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)). For instance, AI is redefining present-day journalism through the introduction of AI-infused workflow systems, the use of data in news stories, and the reorganization of journalistic labor and recruitment (Deuze & Beckett, [Citation 2022](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#); Sun et al., [Citation 2022](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)). AI also has implications in communication, particularly in persuasive communication, in which scholars focus on the interplay between technology and persuasion to investigate how artificial entities like robotics and algorithms (Dehnert & Mongeau, [Citation 2022](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#); Hermann, [Citation 2022](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#); Lee & Liang, [Citation 2018](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#); Siegel et al., [Citation 2009](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)) may influence persuasion across diverse contexts, including political and public opinion (Cohen, [Citation 2021](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)).

In journalism, this technology has reshaped modern investigative reporting, news production, and distribution while targeting users based on their news preferences (Stray, [Citation 2019](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)) by employing cutting-edge AI tools (de Lima-Santos & Ceron, [Citation 2021](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)). Thus far, researchers have identified three phases of computerization in newsrooms: automated information generation, automated content production, and information distribution and audience relations (Sánchez-García et al., [Citation 2023](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)). Each phase influences news organizations’ utilization of machine learning to predict story virality, model audience consumption patterns (Stray, [Citation 2019](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)), and monetize website subscriptions (Goyanes et al., [Citation 2023](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)). Additionally, personalization recommenders, bots, and algorithms have been used by major newsrooms such as *The New York Times*, *Reuters*, *China Morning Post*, and news aggregators like *Google News* and *Yahoo News* (Das et al., [Citation 2007](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#); Sánchez-García et al., [Citation 2023](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)).

Considering the recent academic interest in AI within communication and journalism studies and the implications of its development, it is imperative to reconcile the different research silos that have been evolving with the consolidation of AI in communication-related phenomena. However, little theoretical research has provided and operationalized a definition of AI and a conceptual framework for its empirical application in communication research. We believe this task is timely and relevant as it helps clarify empirical discourses on AI’s nature, use, and effect and their social, political, cultural, and ethical implications in society. This study introduces a wide-ranging working AI scholarly definition in communication research as *the tangible real-world capability of non-human machines or artificial entities to perform, task solve, communicate, interact, and act logically as it occurs with biological humans*. We also propose its theoretical operationalization based on two dimensions: level of *performance* and level of *autonomy*, advancing an elementary conceptual framework drawing on AI’s levels of potential actions or performance the AI may accomplish, including 1) performing tasks, 2) taking decisions, and 3) making predictions; as well as AI’s level of autonomy, or the agency results contingent on the degrees of human input, interaction, or supervision involved.

## Artificial Intelligence: Impact in Journalism and Communication

The concept of AI dates back to 1955 when Prof. John McCarthy defined the scientific procedures of machine learning (de Lima-Santos & Ceron, [Citation 2021](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)). Over the years, this technology has revolutionized how intelligent entities are computed to simplify and perform human tasks, make decisions, and solve problems (Broussard et al., [Citation 2019](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)). For decades, academic focus on AI mainly flourished in computer science with greater concentration on subfields such as “(i) machine learning (Ng & Leung, [Citation 2020](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)); (ii) computer vision- CV (Russell & Norvig, [Citation 2022](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)); (iii) speech recognition (Reddy, [Citation 1976](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)); (iv) natural language processing – NLP (Allen, [Citation 2003](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)); (v) planning, scheduling, and optimization (Wölker & Powell, [Citation 2021](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)); (vi) expert systems (Benfer et al., [Citation 1991](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)); and (vii) robotics” (de Lima-Santos & Ceron, [Citation 2021](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#), p. 3).

The scope of AI, however, has extended globally. Journalists and communication experts, for instance, have used computational-based aid to improve their reporting (Broussard, [Citation 2015](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)), develop more robust investigative journalism (Stray, [Citation 2019](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)), and foster old and new journalistic practices, contributing to better and more informed public opinion (Moran & Shaikh, [Citation 2022](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)). AI automation has proven instrumental to journalism and investigative reporting (de-Lima-Santos & Ceron, [Citation 2021](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#); Borchardt, [Citation 2022](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)). Conversely, the era of pseudo-information (Kim & Gil de Zúñiga, [Citation 2021](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)), post-factual ideas (Moon et al., [Citation 2022](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)) have thrived in part due to the contribution of AI tools that have sparked the emergence of fake news generators, video games, and metaverse (Godulla et al., [Citation 2021](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#); Mattke, [Citation 2018](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)).

In communication, AI is changing social interactions, developing technologies adapted to online and offline communication (Guzman & Lewis, [Citation 2020](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)). AI aims to intelligently replicate human communication abilities aided by computational resources (Frankish, [Citation 2014](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)). Due to the changing dynamics in the connection between AI technologies and communication, new inquiries are being raised on how AI fits into the communication paradigms (Gunkel, [Citation 2012](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)).

Generally, the two principal dimensions in communication technology research are human-computer interaction (HCI) and computer-mediated communication (CMC), which explain how AI might function as a communicator or a channel for human social interactions (Sundar & Nass, [Citation 2000](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)). These dimensions are based on the usability and the ability to interpret social cues by human interlocutors. Recently, these subfields have been classified into human-AI interaction (HAII), a derivative from the Theory of Interactive Media Effects (TIME) model proposed by Sundar (Sundar & Nass, [Citation 2000](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)), and AI-mediated communication \[AIMC\] (Hancock et al., [Citation 2020](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)). Thus, the conceptual advances have facilitated the contextualization of AI as a rise in machine agency, conversational agents, and embodied robots (Beattie et al., [Citation 2020](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#); Westerman et al., [Citation 2020](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)). These versatilities explain how AI assists and streamlines communication (Reeves, [Citation 2016](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)). Nevertheless, the field still needs a simple communication research framework that facilitates the study of AI for such interactions.

With the growing adoption of AI in communication, its functionality has extended from one – to-one encounter to one – to–many, thereby uncovering new assumptions about the human communication process, including deeper socio-emotional interactions (Lee & Sundar, [Citation 2009](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)), human-AI socialization and “friendship” (Brandtzaeg et al., [Citation 2022](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)), and autonomy – as visible in algorithmic assistants, work bots, AI co-authorship, and AI curatorship (Banas et al., [Citation 2022](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#); Duan et al., [Citation 2022](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#); Hepp, [Citation 2020](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)). These facets also highlight AI’s role as an intelligent-agent communicator in contrast to a simple mediator role and the degree of agency it affords humans versus machines in journalism, persuasion, and communication (Dehnert & Mongeau, [Citation 2022](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#); Sundar & Lee, [Citation 2022](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)).

AI is also utilized to resolve issues of hate speech and mis/disinformation online and on social media (Banas et al., [Citation 2022](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)); conduct sentiment analysis, information pattern extraction, visual analysis, and speech recognition (Ryan, [Citation 2020](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#); Vergeer, [Citation 2020](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)). Additionally, communicative bots as artificial companions are becoming more popular among users (Hepp, [Citation 2020](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)) and E-commerce enterprises for sales conversions and learning about customers’ purchasing behaviors via recommender systems (Sun & Zhang, [Citation 2018](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)). However, there are projected benefits to the future scenarios of AI technology and drawbacks to its malicious uses at individual and organizational levels (Nah et al., [Citation 2020](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#); Araujo et al., [Citation 2020](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#); Canavilhas, [Citation 2022](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#); Ciancaglini et al., [Citation 2020](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#); Helberger & Diakopoulos, [Citation 2022](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#); Natale, [Citation 2021](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)).

Despite the advantages of AI, there are ethical, privacy, and authenticity concerns about its deployment (Hermann, [Citation 2022](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#); Kieslich et al., [Citation 2022](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)). For instance, there are issues of lack of transparency and inaccuracy of AI-embedded systems in learning about users’ preferences (Jokinen, [Citation 2015](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)), or the “cold start problem,” where inferences about users may not be accurately drawn (Gope & Jain, [Citation 2017](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#); Lee, [Citation 2020](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)). Some examples are machine learning algorithms used in recommender systems in social media platforms like Tik Tok, Instagram, YouTube, or media streaming services like Netflix, Hulu, Apple TV, etc. There are also uncertainties about increased machine autonomy as AI gains consciousness and becomes self-aware to accelerate attention processing and emulate human-level intelligence like ChatGPT (i.e., GPT-4) and DALL-E (Chella & Manzotti, [Citation 2013](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#); Ng & Leung, [Citation 2020](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)). Several of these adverse effects of AI inspired the launch of the European AI Act in 2021 by the European Commission to provide binding ethical guidelines for private and public integration of AI (Helberger & Diakopoulos, [Citation 2022](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)).

Relatedly, Volovici et al. ([Citation 2022](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)) mention how the abuse of AI in some healthcare systems can propagate prejudices like racism and sexism. Generative AI, a deep learning machine model that creates high-dimensional data from simple prompts, like texts, images, or workable prototypes in response to users’ commands (i.e., ChatGPT, and Dall-E), has been identified as a potential threat to journalists (Jamil, [Citation 2021](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#); Ciancaglini et al., [Citation 2020](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)) and marketers due to the ability of AI to perform tasks accurately and quickly (Pavlik, [Citation 2023](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)). Although AI tools are expected to reduce the costs of investigative journalism, current meta-journalistic conversations begin to suggest otherwise due to the uptake of algorithmic processes that undermine human involvement in news production (Borchardt et al., [Citation 2019](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#); Guzman, [Citation 2018](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#); Moran & Shaikh, [Citation 2022](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)). Likewise, there are predictions of the death of journalism values as journalists grapple with over-reliance on robots in the newsrooms, raising ethical debates on how AI is appropriated in the news (Moran & Shaikh, [Citation 2022](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)).

## Scholarly Definition of Artificial Intelligence in Communication Research

The study of AI in communication has relied on broad descriptions from computer science and engineering, sociology, and legal studies as “learning algorithm used to approximate some form of intelligence operating within computing machines” (Ninness & Ninness, [Citation 2020](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#), p. 100). In some cases, AI has been subsumed as automation in media use and production (Hepp, [Citation 2020](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)) and categorized as general AI, which explains intelligent systems designed to conduct limited tasks as opposed to possessing similar or higher human intelligence (Ryan, [Citation 2020](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)). The role of AI in communication and newly proposed conceptual parameters – human-AI interaction (HAII) and AI-mediated communication, are discussed by scholars based on factors like source orientation and social exchanges optimized by machine learning (Guzman & Lewis, [Citation 2020](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#); Sundar & Lee, [Citation 2022](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)).

Furthermore, there is a lack of operationalization of different constructs to assess the impact of AI on democracy, news media, social and political attitudes, and perceptions such as public trust (Chen & Wen, [Citation 2021](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)).

We define AI as *the tangible real-world capability of non-human machines or artificial entities to perform, task solve, communicate, interact, and act logically as it occurs with biological humans*. AI may not solely depend on human intelligence to define its existence, and we argue that its operationalization is based on two parameters: level of *performance* and level of *autonomy*. Accordingly, this study advances an elementary conceptual framework drawing on AI’s levels of performance, or the actions AI may accomplish, which include 1) performing tasks, 2) making decisions, and 3) making predictions, as well as AI’s level of autonomy, as the agency results contingent on the degrees of human input, interaction, or supervision involved. Like humans, AI will disclose different levels of intelligence based on its performance and autonomy capabilities.

## AI as a Conceptual Framework in Communication Research

Drawing on current communication studies and building on our definition of AI, we argue that AI embraces a notion that may be categorized into two levels: 1) the depth of analysis that AI can perform specific tasks, make decisions, communicate, interact, and forecast results, resulting in unique output, and 2) the degree to which AI requires or does not require human supervision or interaction. Based on these two features, our framework for explicating AI in communication research stands as follows:

### Level of performance

This level of analysis, which is consistently but dispersedly discussed in the literature as an inherent feature of AI (Gunkel, [Citation 2012](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#); Lee et al., [Citation 2023](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#); Park et al., [Citation 2021](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)), is implicitly appraised by extant research but lacks the consistent operationalization we seek to present next. It entails the potential actions of AI, including 1) performing tasks, 2) making decisions, and 3) making predictions. These three categories should be understood as complex actions with different levels of interaction and mutual feedback, as well as an initial approach to understand and advance a historically primitive framework of AI in communication (*see*).

Performing a task, for instance, can be theoretically assumed to be a simple action for AI, but as we proceed to deciding or predicting, the level of complexity increases. The level of complexity rises as the potential connections between these activities are also triggered: for example, decisions based on predictions (i.e., Gmail and Outlook text predictions, Grammarly, Google editor) or predictions based on decisions (i.e., Canva and Google search engine). What we argue here is that the output of AI depends on the intricacies of an AI interaction between its performative levels, 1) performing tasks, 2) making decisions, and 3) making predictions to produce results, and the quality of inputs an AI initially receives or its level of autonomy.

AI’s level of performance can be understood through automated decision-making, which describes actions conducted using advanced technologies and without human involvement to make data-driven decisions (Dodge & Kitchin, [Citation 2007](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#); European Commission, [Citation 2018](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)). In other words, algorithms are utilized to gather, process, and generate data for making decisions in some aspects, including political, cultural, economic, and ethical (Elish & Boyd, [Citation 2018](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#); Kitchin, [Citation 2017](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)). Gunkel ([Citation 2012](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)) and Natale ([Citation 2021](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)) emphasized that AI should be viewed from a technical and material functioning of computing technologies or machine intelligence, which is pivotal in modeling the relationship between humans and machines.

In digital journalism, social bots are employed to create a scenario where AI technologies are active producers of communication, including news content (Natale, [Citation 2021](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)). Tong and Walther ([Citation 2011](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)) mention the use of algorithms and search engines for data mining news leads and crafting prose to refine news stories. This indicates a practice of computational journalism where algorithmic tools are employed for tracking topics on public affairs (Appelgren & Nygren, [Citation 2014](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)), analyzing big data sets (Andersen, [Citation 2018](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)), fact-checking falsified information, and restructuring journalistic processes (Lewis et al., [Citation 2020](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)). Some examples include Open Refine (openrefine.org) and Dedupe.io (DataMade, [Citation 2016](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)).

Furthermore, AI may perform tasks and make predictions in aggregated forms. Vergeer ([Citation 2020](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)) reveals that the gatekeeping function of journalists is disrupted with the use of machine learning algorithms to build paywalls and recommender systems that help journalists learn about the audience’s interests (Shaw et al., [Citation 2021](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#); Gruszczynski & Wagner, [Citation 2017](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)). It makes decisions using proprietary content management systems and statistical programs. Automated news is becoming a trend in digital journalism (Broussard, [Citation 2015](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)), with several machine-written news pieces being created across multiple languages within minutes (Dörr, [Citation 2016](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#); Hansen et al., [Citation 2017](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)). Nevertheless, the importance of AI is related to automated news articles, as well as newsroom planning, post-production, and optimization via chatbots and other AIs such as Quakebot (Salaverría & de Lima-Santos, [Citation 2020](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)), ChatGPT, and Dall-E (Moran & Shaik, [Citation 2022](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)).

Drawing on our AI framework, we argue machine-human interaction and online communication is also changing. Institutions are increasingly adopting machine agents or chatbots to carry out a variety of communicative tasks previously designated to humans, such as customer support (Adam et al., [Citation 2021](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)), scheduling appointments (Srinivas & Ravindran, [Citation 2018](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)), and personal banking (Umamaheswari et al., [Citation 2023](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)). AI has also permeated everyday human conversations that can manage people’s mental health (Adam et al., [Citation 2021](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)), convey nonverbal expressions with emojis (Prada et al., [Citation 2018](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)), introduce identity shifts (Gonzales & Hancock, [Citation 2008](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)), and foster relationships (Tong & Walther, [Citation 2011](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)). AI models like ChatGPT and DALL-E (Zhang et.al., [Citation 2023](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)), developed by research lab OpenAI, have made headlines for their ability to function as meaningful AI. Only levels of performance and the level of autonomy will dictate how this AI will affect the world in the future.

Following the analyses of AI’s functioning, a theoretical evaluation of these AI activities denotes that they should be understood as multilayered actions that require the interaction of single, dual, or triple activities at different levels. Moreover, the level of intricacy increases as mutual feedback and potential connections between these AI actions are systematically triggered. Digital streaming service Netflix, for instance, provides customized recommendations based on users’ viewing history and likes (Sun & Zhang, [Citation 2018](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)).

Major digital companies like Google, Alibaba, Amazon, and Apple have introduced conversational AI to revolutionize information systems. These bots are integrated as AI assistants such as Alexa (Amazon), Siri (Apple), Google Assistant, and Bard (Google’s chat GPT-like AI), which can process instructions, engage in chitchat, and allow users to access information (Sun & Zhang, [Citation 2018](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)). shows the majority of present-day AI activities, portraying the three integral actions and their relationships.

Deuze and Beckett ([Citation 2022](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)) reference how AI-infused systems create news stories and gather public issues that resonate most among audiences through various websites or applications. There are cases of Twitter bot indicators - *Botometer* and *Tweetbotornot* - performing tasks like detecting automated accounts that collate tweets used to promote political discourses (Martini et al., [Citation 2021](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)). With the rapid advancement in AI technology, sophisticated predictions are made using, for instance, voice recognition applications to detect health-related issues (Schulz, [Citation 2017](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)), or algorithms to discover hidden trends from big data that might provide insights into relevant news topics (Stray, [Citation 2019](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)).

Hansen et al. ([Citation 2017](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)) and Marconi and Siegman ([Citation 2017](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)) mentioned the impact of machine intelligence in monitoring global feeds, drawing patterns from big data sets and collating the results in writing a news story that can be shared with the audience. Today, the prevalence of AI technologies has made such theorizing a reality with several examples of machine learning used in interpersonal and computer-mediated contexts (Beattie et al., [Citation 2020](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#); Westerman et al., [Citation 2020](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)) and by news organizations (Marconi & Siegman, [Citation 2017](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#); Schmitt, [Citation 2019](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#); Wölker & Powell, [Citation 2021](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)). However, these AI’s performance levels are not exclusive to journalism or communication disciplines.

### Level of autonomy

This level of analysis provides a nuance on perceptions of the potential of AI-Human interaction and has been a debate in the literature (Hancock et al., [Citation 2020](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#); Milano et al., [Citation 2020](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)). Theoretically assumed to be of high or low levels of autonomy, AI’s autonomy discerns the agency result of the potential degree of AI or human input, interaction, or supervision involved. Fortunati ([Citation 2018](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)) references how AI communicative bots are designed as autonomously operating systems to facilitate quasi-communication between humans, and AI possesses different interfaces of communicative autonomy. The function of communicative bots depends on datafication and continuous feedback, which determines if a system is partially or fully automated, as well as the variation of oversight it affords individuals (Araujo et al., [Citation 2020](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#); Esposito, [Citation 2017](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)). So, the larger the amount of data processing capabilities of any given AI, the higher the automation and extent of mediatization (Andersen, [Citation 2018](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)) and, ultimately, autonomy.

Some AI tools are developed to be “functionally automatic, to act when triggered without any regular human intervention or oversight” (Gillespie et al., [Citation 2014](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#), p. 170) This indicates high visualization of information from databases or the internet. AI companions such as Apple’s Siri, Amazon’s Alexa, Microsoft’s Cortana, Google’s Assistant, and Shazam are autonomous systems that meet human socio-communication needs (Diakopoulos, [Citation 2016](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#); Hepp, [Citation 2020](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#); Selwyn & Gallo Cordoba, [Citation 2022](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)). Nevertheless, these AI tools provide various degrees of control for users. Shazam, for instance, gives users extended control over their music consumption because it is designed to identify songs via a smartphone’s microphone and propose music using search results saved in users’ databases (Hepp, [Citation 2020](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)). This conceptualization of AI-enabled interaction, thus, deals with humans’ control over AI and not the opposite. Results of a fully supervised AI technology may imply different societal, economic, or human risks, assuming that if humans fully control AI technologies, this may influence fewer risking outputs, as opposed to a fully autonomous AI technology close to the cutting-edge technological singularity.

## Discussion

Many news agencies employ AI tools to enhance their users’ experiences (Lee et al., [Citation 2023](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)) and improve automated news production and distribution (Deuze & Beckett, [Citation 2022](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#); Moran & Shaikh, [Citation 2022](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)), redefining our society (Barredo-Ibáñez et al., [Citation 2021](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#); Dehnert & Mongeau, [Citation 2022](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#); Siegel et al., [Citation 2009](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)). AI represents a fundamental role in many strands of society, from industries to scholarship, including communication as a field. Thus, it is crucial to define its core processes and provide a framework aiming at capturing the broadness of its application in communication research. Furthermore, as AI grows rapidly, predominant theories on communication research only provide a partial perspective of AI’s framework. Hence, our proposed definition of AI broadly conceives the complexity of AI based on its level of performance and autonomy and the procedures involved. This study provides a holistic scholarly definition of AI as the tangible real-world capability of non-human machines to perform, task solve, communicate, and interact as it occurs with humans.

AI’s levels of performance and autonomy situate theoretical perspectives of human-machine interaction and the variants of the practices conducted by AI. These classifications will help scholars and users reimagine the different uses of AI systems and their outcomes for various issues. Scholars, for instance, could investigate the potential and effects of computational journalism to deepen conversations on AI’s formative role in the newsroom and examine audiences’ reaction to AI-generated news vs. journalist-written news. Future studies may explore the psychological effects of AI literacy on individuals’ expectations of how an algorithm’s performance might help them distinguish between fake and real news, thereby increasing fake news literacy among audiences who consume news from social media or alternative media, which are known as hotspots for the spread of misinformation (Lee et al., [Citation 2023](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#); Mason et al., [Citation 2018](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)).

Moreso, there is an uncanniness in how AI can accentuate social and digital disparities. With the growing popularity of generative AI models like Chat GPT, GPT-3, and GPT-4, there is an opportunity to leverage AI’s performance and autonomy in designing empirical studies on the personalization and human-like conversational metrics of AI agents to explore the emotional intelligence in human-AI interactions and its impact on users’ perceptions. Following the distinctions of AI, it is clear that media and communication studies will pose different research questions and attempt to address debates on the communicative relationship between humans and AI, including ethical contentions.

Numerous AI challenges need to be faced by academics and policymakers. Diverse AI-like communicative robots, for instance, are based on the premise of the accumulation of individual private data, so there is a need for a clearer understanding of what data is public or private when AI is embedded in users’ social spaces (Lutz & Tamó-Larrieux, [Citation 2020](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)) as developers make specific trade-off decisions that may threaten the ethical design of AI tools. Additionally, it is critical to bring about the notion of AI *accountability*, that is, who bears the responsibility for AI intrusion in users’ private spaces (Gunkel, [Citation 2018](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)). AI systems may run the risk of undermining transparency for users concerning the machine learning process (Liao et al., [Citation 2020](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)) and disclosure of the methods of data collection and the extent of individuating information collected (Sundar et al., [Citation 2013](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)); tracking their data, and monitoring their digital footprints such as interests and media use patterns.

Over time, different institutions like the European Union continually address such issues through policymaking (Canavilhas, [Citation 2022](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)) and the U.S. Congress is also considering AI’s implications to society and the health of US democracy. The most recent case is the Tik Tok Congressional Hearing on privacy invasion, data security, political propaganda, and child safety (Shepardson & Ayyub, [Citation 2023](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#); Tolentino, [Citation 2023](https://www-tandfonline-com.sire.ub.edu/doi/full/10.1080/#)). Despite the risks and vulnerabilities caused by the advancement of AI, this Forum article aims to present a standard working definition of AI in communication and provide a conceptual framework for understanding how AI models are structured, extending research on the examination of their future influence in society.
