Hello 



Archives, museums, libraries and galleries curate, characterize and describe human knowledge affecting both, positively and negatively, the representation of specific social groups in their machine-readable databases and collections from which knowledge is derived. 

The misrepresentation of such social groups in digital datasets is a problematic subject that has attracted attention from different fields, particularly in the last decade. 

For this reason, their role must be understood using historically attuned technological and conceptual tools, in order to address the epistemological injustices caused and prevent new ones. 

In this context, the PhD research proposal entitled 'Participatory Design and Neuro-Symbolic Artificial intelligence for Digital Historical Archives', which is yet to be reviewed, is intended to address epistemic injustice in Francoist repression databases as a use case.

By focusing on five digital archives related to such historical period, this research aims to contribute to how historical information is curated, accessed, and represented with particular attention to gender perspectives, intersectionalities, and underrepresented voices.
