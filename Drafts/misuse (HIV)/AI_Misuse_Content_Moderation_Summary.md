# Tackling AI Misuse for Sexual Content Involving Minors: A Research Summary

**Date:** February 19, 2026

## Summary

Tackling AI misuse for sexual content involving minors requires multi-layered safety and moderation strategies that can be developed through participatory design, co-creation, and user-centered design (UCD) methods involving diverse stakeholders. Generative AI increases attack scale and speed, lowering barriers to creating harmful deepfakes (Kelley et al., 2025). Defense leverages AI for detection at scale, combining natural language processing and image analysis to identify harmful material in real-time (Budhewar et al., 2025). However, effective solutions must be co-designed with moderators, survivors, safety experts, and affected communities using participatory approaches that doesn't align with market values, that challenge power structures and enable non-expert stakeholders to provide direct input on technology design ([[The Participatory Turn in AI Design Theoretical Foundations and the Current State of Practice.md]]). Co-creation processes facilitate creative cooperation between stakeholders and designers across the entire design span, ensuring tools address real needs while maintaining safety-first principles learned from vulnerable populations research ([[Co-creation and the new landscapes of design.md]]; Orlowski et al., 2015). Platform governance must address nonconsensual image-based abuse through robust policies and tools developed via multi-stakeholder participatory workshops (Henry & Witt, 2021). Deepfake detection using advanced neural networks achieves high accuracy (Lyu, 2022), but UCD methods ensure interfaces are usable, transparent, and protect moderator wellbeing. Solutions require AI-powered detection systems, co-created moderation processes developed through iterative prototyping with end-users, transparent decision-making interfaces designed using UCD principles, user reputation scoring, and human-AI hybrid decision-making developed through participatory action research involving affected communities ([[Reformulating computational social science with citizen social science_ the case of a community-based mental health care research - Humanities and Social Sciences Communications.md]]) to protect vulnerable users.

## Neurosymbolic AI Approaches

While no papers specifically address neurosymbolic AI for detecting AI-generated sexual content involving minors, **neurosymbolic approaches show promise** for content moderation and safety applications. Neurosymbolic AI combines neural networks (pattern recognition) with symbolic reasoning (rule-based logic), offering advantages for trustworthy detection systems.

**Key Finding:** Gaur & Sheth (2024) propose the **CREST framework** (Consistency, Reliability, Explainability, Safety) using neurosymbolic methods. This framework integrates statistical AI (neural networks) with symbolic AI (knowledge graphs, rules) to build trustworthy systems. For content moderation, neurosymbolic approaches could:

- **Enhance explainability**: Symbolic reasoning provides interpretable rules for why content is flagged
- **Improve reliability**: Combining learned patterns with explicit safety rules reduces false positives/negatives
- **Enable consistency**: Symbolic knowledge ensures consistent application of moderation policies
- **Support safety**: Explicit safety constraints encoded symbolically complement neural detection

**Relevance**: While current research focuses on general content moderation using neural networks, neurosymbolic frameworks like CREST could be adapted for detecting harmful AI-generated content by combining:
- Neural networks for pattern recognition (deepfake detection, text analysis)
- Symbolic rules encoding legal/ethical constraints (child protection laws, platform policies)
- Knowledge graphs representing relationships between users, content, and violations

**Research Gap**: Direct application of neurosymbolic AI to child safety content detection remains underexplored but represents a promising direction for future work.

## User-Centered Design (UCD) Software Development

**Research Gap**: No papers specifically address UCD methodologies for developing content moderation software targeting AI-generated sexual content involving minors. However, **participatory design and user-centered approaches from related domains** offer valuable principles applicable to this context.

**Key Findings from Related Domains:**

1. **Participatory Design for Vulnerable Populations** (Orlowski et al., 2015): Systematic review of consumer involvement in technology-based youth mental health interventions demonstrates that **participatory research methods** help shape intervention design. Key principles include:
   - Involving end-users (youth, survivors, moderators) throughout the design process
   - Co-design workshops and iterative prototyping
   - Addressing safety and privacy concerns from the start

2. **Safety-First Design Principles** (Uusitalo, 2018): Research on designing mobile services for women experiencing intimate partner violence identified critical UCD principles:
   - **Safety and privacy**: Protecting users from threats (password protection, hidden content, security information)
   - **Validation and empowerment**: Supporting users' agency and decision-making
   - **Encouragement to reach out**: Facilitating access to support resources

**Application to Content Moderation Tools:**

UCD approaches for moderation software should involve:
- **Moderator-centered design**: Involving content moderators in tool development to reduce burnout, improve efficiency, and enhance decision-making
- **Survivor-centered design**: Engaging survivors of online abuse to understand reporting mechanisms, support needs, and safety requirements
- **Child-centered design**: Involving youth (age-appropriately) to understand their online experiences and protection needs
- **Multi-stakeholder participation**: Including moderators, safety experts, legal advisors, and affected communities

**Design Considerations:**
- **Moderator wellbeing**: Tools should reduce exposure to harmful content, provide breaks, and offer mental health support
- **Transparency**: Clear explanations of AI decisions, allowing moderators to understand and override automated flags
- **Efficiency**: Streamlined workflows for reviewing flagged content, reducing decision fatigue
- **Accountability**: Audit trails and documentation for moderation decisions
- **Accessibility**: Tools usable by moderators with diverse technical skills and languages

**Research Need**: Dedicated UCD research is needed to develop moderation interfaces specifically for detecting and handling AI-generated sexual content involving minors, incorporating lessons from related safety-critical domains.
