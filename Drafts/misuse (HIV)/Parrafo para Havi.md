**Humanities Research Question**: How can digital humanities scholars understand and address 
the misuse of AI to create or spread sexual content on social networks, particularly when minors are involved?. This project addresses a critical gap in digital humanities research: developing **participatory design and co-creation methodologies** that enable humanities scholars to collaborate directly with interested communities—including moderators, abuse survivors, safety experts, and cultural stakeholders—in **co-creating both the software development processes and curatorial practices** for AI-powered content moderation tools. Through iterative co-design workshops, stakeholders collectively determine how content is classified, organized, preserved, and managed, ensuring **curatorial decisions** reflect cultural nuances, historical understanding, and multilingual diversity essential to humanistic inquiry ([[The Participatory Turn in AI Design Theoretical Foundations and the Current State of Practice.md]]; [[Co-creation and the new landscapes of design.md]]).

## Summary

Tackling AI misuse for sexual content involving minors requires multi-layered safety and moderation strategies that can be developed through participatory design, co-creation, and user-centered design (UCD) methods involving diverse stakeholders. Generative AI increases attack scale and speed, lowering barriers to creating harmful deepfakes (Kelley et al., 2025). Defense leverages AI for detection at scale, combining natural language processing and image analysis to identify harmful material in real-time (Budhewar et al., 2025). However, effective solutions must be co-designed with moderators, survivors, safety experts, and affected communities using participatory approaches that doesn't align with market values, that challenge power structures and enable non-expert stakeholders to provide direct input on technology design ([[The Participatory Turn in AI Design Theoretical Foundations and the Current State of Practice.md]]). Co-creation processes facilitate creative cooperation between stakeholders and designers across the entire design span, ensuring tools address real needs while maintaining safety-first principles learned from vulnerable populations research ([[Co-creation and the new landscapes of design.md]]; Orlowski et al., 2015). Platform governance must address nonconsensual image-based abuse through robust policies and tools developed via multi-stakeholder participatory workshops (Henry & Witt, 2021). Deepfake detection using advanced neural networks achieves high accuracy (Lyu, 2022), but UCD methods ensure interfaces are usable, transparent, and protect moderator wellbeing. Solutions require AI-powered detection systems, co-created moderation processes developed through iterative prototyping with end-users, transparent decision-making interfaces designed using UCD principles, user reputation scoring, and human-AI hybrid decision-making developed through participatory action research involving affected communities ([[Reformulating computational social science with citizen social science_ the case of a community-based mental health care research - Humanities and Social Sciences Communications.md]]) to protect vulnerable users.

## Resumen (Español)

Abordar el mal uso de la IA para contenido sexual con menores requiere estrategias de seguridad y moderación multicapa que pueden ser desarrolladas mediante diseño participativo, co-creación y métodos de diseño centrado en el usuario (UCD) que involucren a todas las partes interesadas. La IA generativa aumenta la escala y velocidad de los ataques, reduciendo barreras para crear deepfakes dañinos (Kelley et al., 2025). La defensa aprovecha la IA para detección a escala, combinando procesamiento de lenguaje natural y análisis de imágenes para identificar material dañino en tiempo real (Budhewar et al., 2025). Sin embargo, las soluciones efectivas deben ser co-diseñadas con moderadores, supervivientes, expertos en seguridad y comunidades afectadas usando enfoques participativos que no estén alineadas con las lógicas del mercado, que desafíen las estructuras de poder y permitan que las partes interesadas no expertas proporcionen aportes directos sobre el diseño tecnológico ([[The Participatory Turn in AI Design Theoretical Foundations and the Current State of Practice.md]]). Los procesos de co-creación facilitan la cooperación creativa entre partes interesadas y diseñadores a lo largo de todo el proceso de diseño, asegurando que las herramientas aborden necesidades reales mientras mantienen principios de seguridad primero aprendidos de la investigación con poblaciones vulnerables ([[Co-creation and the new landscapes of design.md]]; Orlowski et al., 2015). La gobernanza de plataformas debe abordar el abuso basado en imágenes no consensuadas mediante políticas y herramientas robustas desarrolladas a través de **talleres participativos multi-stakeholder** (Henry & Witt, 2021). La detección de deepfakes usando redes neuronales avanzadas logra alta precisión (Lyu, 2022), pero los **métodos UCD** aseguran que las interfaces sean usables, transparentes y protejan el bienestar de los moderadores. Las soluciones requieren sistemas de detección impulsados por IA, **procesos de moderación co-creados** desarrollados mediante prototipado iterativo con usuarios finales, interfaces de toma de decisiones transparentes diseñadas usando principios UCD, puntuación de reputación de usuarios, y toma de decisiones híbrida humano-IA desarrollada mediante **investigación-acción participativa** involucrando comunidades afectadas ([[Reformulating computational social science with citizen social science_ the case of a community-based mental health care research - Humanities and Social Sciences Communications.md]]) para proteger usuarios vulnerables.
