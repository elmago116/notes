
# Introduction

This study is made as part of a PhD proposal in the context of HerStory&NeSyAI, a three-year research project (PID2023-147673OB-I00) that connects women’s history with Neuro‑Symbolic Artificial Intelligence (NeSyAI) through an Information Architecture (IA) lens and user-centered design practices. Its goal is to mitigate gender bias in information systems [[Assessing knowledge organization systems from a gender perspective Wikipedia taxonomy.pdf#page=1&selection=0,0,3,19|Assessing knowledge organization systems from a gender perspective Wikipedia taxonomy, p.124]] by improving how data about women and misrepresented groups is searched, navigated, organized, labeled, visualized, and narrated. As its goal states, mitigating such biases and informational gaps is not just a matter of advancing in the semantic and IA theory and practices; these need to be complemented with human co-creation processes [[What is Human-Centered about Human-Centered AI_ A Map of the Research Landscape _ Proceedings of the 2023 CHI Conference on Human Factors in Computing Systems.pdf#page=1&annotation=6794R|What is Human-Centered about Human-Centered AI_ A Map of the Research Landscape _ Proceedings of the 2023 CHI Conference on Human Factors in Computing Systems, p.1]] to be coherent to humanistic points of view, enabling enriched cognitive and emotional readings, improving data dissemination and use, and fostering inclusive knowledge production given that "it is obvious that certain material advantages will generate the envisaged epistemological advantage" [[Epistemic Injustice  Power and the Ethics of Knowing.pdf#page=158&annotation=2386R|Epistemic Injustice _ Power and the Ethics of Knowing -- Fricker, Miranda(Author) -- Repr, 2011 -- Oxford University Press, USA -- 9780191519307 -- 82f948343138858a134cb4eb8c94a703 -- Anna’s Archive, p.147]]. Participatory design, User-centered, human-centered and similars forms co-creation are a "design approach aimed at developing technologies with close involvement from stakeholders - especially those most affected by the end result, most often users [[Participatory_design_A_systematic_review_and_insights_for_future_practice.pdf#page=1&annotation=211R|Participatory_design_A_systematic_review_and_insights_for_future_practice, p.1]]. However, substantial ambiguity persists regarding a comprehensive understanding of these fields [[Human_AI_Co_Creation_A_Framework_for_Collaborative_Design_in_Intelligent_Systems.pdf]], especially in the conceptual and methodological frameworks [[Participatory_design_A_systematic_review_and_insights_for_future_practice.pdf]] raising important ethical questions given that it's not the same aim if "the human might be the subject of AI algorithmic study, the user of AI products, an agent in the design of the AI system itself and so on [[What is Human-Centered about Human-Centered AI_ A Map of the Research Landscape _ Proceedings of the 2023 CHI Conference on Human Factors in Computing Systems.pdf#page=1&annotation=6794R|What is Human-Centered about Human-Centered AI_ A Map of the Research Landscape _ Proceedings of the 2023 CHI Conference on Human Factors in Computing Systems, p.1]]. This is why it’s important to recognize the conceptual structure of the field and build strong methodological foundations to make the development of this technological processes truly a user-centered designed one [[What is Human-Centered about Human-Centered AI_ A Map of the Research Landscape _ Proceedings of the 2023 CHI Conference on Human Factors in Computing Systems]].  
## Methodology: 

This study consists of a scoping review based on SALSA (Search, Appraisal, Synthesis, Analysis) framework combined with a bibliometric technique called scientific maps [[Science mapping software tools_ Review, analysis, and cooperative study among tools]] to map the conceptual structure of the interdisciplinary field formed between the search interests. The SALSA framework guided the systematic exploration of academic literature through four sequential phases. (1) The Search phase utilized Perplexity AI for assistance [[Codina-Buscadores-alternativos - perplexity - note]]  with search equations and refining results [[ChatGPT como apoyo a las systematic scoping reviews- integrando la inteligencia artificial con el framework SALSA.pdf]] to get a significant sample of literature, both the actual documents and the most complete metadata that the search could gather to apply the bibliometric techniques effectively. This systematic data extraction from Scopus (2,657 records) and Web of Science (613 records) gathered from 2014 to 2025, yielding 3,270 unique records after deduplication. (2) The appraisal phase involved systematic evaluation and metadata corpus refinement, while the (3) synthesis step examined both general field structure and project-specific thematic positioning. For the bibliometric analysis, scientific mapping techniques [[Literature Reviews Modern Methods for Investigating Scientific and Technological Knowledge.pdf]] were employed using SciMAT software [[SciMAT_ A new science mapping analysis software tool]], co-word analysis to examine keyword co-occurrence networks [[Co-word analysis as a tool for describing the network of interactions between basic and technological research  The case of polymer chemsitry - Scientometrics]]  with a curated sample of 45,650 normalized keywords extracted from the academic records. The database’s search equations used focused, proximity, and broad operators such as `W/n`, `NEAR/n`, wild cards `*`, and field-specific labels to ensure both extension and theme presence without manually selecting the inclusion of metadata in the analysis and preventing bias. The analysis chose the simple centers clustering algorithm with inclusion index normalization [[Science mapping software tools_ Review, analysis, and cooperative study among tools]] [[An approach for detecting, quantifying, and visualizing the evolution of a research field - A practical application to the Fuzzy Sets Theory field]], generating a themed network with 14,151 nodes and 231,319 edges (Callon’s centrality and density measures) in two five-year periods (2014/2019 - P1 and 2020-2025- P2) to grasp what are called the strategic zones. Then the clusters formed with themed keywords are positioned into four quadrants: motor themes, specialized themes, emerging or declining themes, and basic and transversal themes [[Industry 4.0  a perspective based on bibliometric analysis]] to describe the conceptual structure of the field in the last 10 years.  

The methodology enabled both quantitative mapping of the field’s conceptual structure and a qualitative identification of relevant projects and user-centered design or participatory design methodologies in GLAM (galleries, libraries, archives, and museums) and semantic web technologies and similars.  

# General analysis of the conceptual structure 

The bibliometric analysis reveals distinct conceptual structures between the two periods. Expressed in the strategic diagrams, it shows differences in cluster distribution across the four quadrants, as figures 3 and 4 presentction (decrease from 3 specialized themes in P1 to only 1 in P2), a basic theme expansion (significant increase from 3 basic themes in P1 to 6 in P2), and an emerging/declining stability (slight increase from 12 to 13 emerging/declining themes). This is partially due to the decrease in density measure as the strength of the internal ties given the keywords’ co-occurrence patterns in the sample. As the majority of clusters are placed in the emergent/declining zone, a disciplinary dispersion trend, or a low concept standardization dynamic, needs to be considered when approaching searches on such topics. 

Keywords in the motor field (P1), with a strong structure, are related to minority culture heritage in folk song creation, regional dresses, national identity, and ethnic sports. The ones in the specialized themes are also named heritage music, art, dancing (with very specific Chinese keywords), marine theme (from the ecological, administrative, and heritage points of view). Other clusters are less dense and use keywords related to social innovation and sustainability, co-creation and value co-creation, identity (related with ethnic terms and social image). In P2, there’s only one cluster, the 0-P2 with maximum density and almost cero centrality, and it contains some of the central search terms integrating knowledge graphs, participative design methodologies, cultural heritage, and women. This cluster is also present on the P1 but in the emergent/declining zone almost with the same structure except for the women keyword, which is also present in the P1 but without strong relations doesn’t even appear in the strategical graph. This is an interesting finding that will be discussed in the second part of the analysis, which is about the localization of the HerStory NeSy AI project-specific terms. 

The presence of different subjects beyond the scope of original search terms is due to the SciMAT data process and the search strategies used. Networks are built from keyword co-occurrence patterns, not only using their frequency. Documents containing search terms also contain other terms, and their patterns might be stronger than the intended ones; that’s why the second part of the analysis is needed.  

# Project-specific conceptual positioning and interpretation  

To identify the strategic positions of topics related to the HerStory NeSy AI project. The  task was to evidence the presence of this project’s topics in the structures of the strategic maps and clusters, based on the initial search terms and obtained metadata-keywords (author’s and database’s). The keywords directly related to the topics of interest have a presence in 13 clusters in P1 (emerging and specialized zones) and in 16 clusters in P2, following the general perspective reading. 

Regarding search terms related to (1) historical heritage and GLAM context, was observed that clusters containing intangible cultural heritage, cultural heritage, and both virtual libraries and museums are located across all zones in P1, while in P2 they remain in emerging/ declining themes, showing generalized presence across clusters. For (2) digital humanities or specific type-semantic technologies, was observed they appear in very specific clusters in both review moments. They are part of clusters 0, 9, 11, and 17 in P1, across a broad spectrum of densities, and clusters 0, 7, and 13 in P2, each in a different zone: specialized, emerging/ declining, and basic (in sequential order). Keywords found include knowledge graphs, semantics, metadata models and aggregation, RDF schemata, linked open data, among others. For topics related to (3) gender, minorities, and intersectionalities, keywords such as ethnic minorities, minority cultural heritage, social justice, gender, women issues, women's rights, lack of diversity, identity were found. There is no clear localization; these are transversal themes across clusters, so the reading corresponds with the general analysis performed in the previous section. Regarding keywords related to (4) UCD, we observe this term and others such as co-creation, social innovation, design activism, participatory approach, usability testing, community-based participation. These are also located following the general reading. 

Greater visibility was expected for keywords that are conceptually more encompassing than those found, although the number of co-occurrences is higher, the inclusion algorithm and simple centers allow visualization of those with stronger relationships. Instead, these keywords can be understood as bridges between the clusters, and this can be explored in future research.  

# Conclusions  

The scientific maps methodology was able to show that the interdisciplinary fields can be explored through keyword networks. This is, nevertheless, a expansive and pixelated image of the themes present in the production field formed in the intersection of heritage-GLAM sector, gender, minorities and intersectionalities, UCD and Semantic web technologies and their connection to AI. Findings will serve as clear context for deepening the project’s theoretical framework through an interdisciplinary lens. Although the search terms as keywords weren’t as prominent as expected, they enabled latent relationships within them to be seen. In P1 (2014-2019), 13 clusters contained interest-related keywords, and in P2 (2020-2025), 16 clusters contained them (in general terms) the clusters evidenced different subjects from marine heritage to ethnobotanics, which came to the map as limits of the actual search. Digital humanities technologies, including the specific type of technologies of interest (Knowledge graphs, RDF, open linked data, and AI related) specific clusters with varying densities and strategic positions, were part of at least 5 clusters across periods. And gender/intersectionality themes were transversal across clusters without clear localization. Finally, UCD methodologies were distributed following the general thematic patterns. One cluster was identified with a similar structure to the original search (0-P1 and 0-P2). In P1, it was either emergent or declining, but in P2, its density grew to become a specialized theme, besides adding a keyword (woman) to the conceptual structure. an important one, to the HerStoy NeSy AI project.
