# DCMI 2026 — three abstract drafts (short paper, participation / co-creation)

Submissions: English; short papers 4–5 pages single-spaced plus references; aligned with DCMI 2026 CfP theme and topic areas (human-centered metadata, cultural/social dimensions, FAIR/CARE, ethics, metadata and AI).

## Angle 1 — Participatory metadata services: from co-design to governance

**Title (working):** Participatory indexing as value-aligned metadata: a co-design lens on academic digital libraries

**Abstract**

Metadata standards and controlled vocabularies are often framed as neutral infrastructure, yet whose terms count—and who may apply them—directly shapes discoverability, trust, and inclusion. This short paper argues that participatory indexing services, when co-designed with potential users, are a concrete instance of meaning-driven metadata: descriptors encode not only document topics but negotiated priorities of communities and institutions. Drawing on participatory design workshops and the implementation of user-contributed indexing aligned with controlled vocabularies in academic and cultural heritage digital library settings, we outline a compact framework linking (i) levels of participation, (ii) vocabulary and policy constraints, and (iii) ethical risks such as asymmetry of voice and epistemic marginalization. We relate this framework to DCMI’s emphasis on human-centered metadata, ethics, and trustworthy representation, and identify implications for evaluating participatory metadata tools beyond usability alone. The contribution is methodological and practice-oriented: a reusable structure for reporting co-created indexing services as value-sensitive metadata systems rather than as ad hoc features.

**Keywords (draft):** participatory indexing; co-design; digital libraries; controlled vocabularies; human-centered metadata; ethics

## Angle 2 — Lightweight research knowledge graphs for participatory archives

**Title (working):** Small-scale research knowledge graphs as bridges between participatory archives and the linked open data cloud

**Abstract**

Large research knowledge graphs and central hubs facilitate discovery across domains, but many humanities and memory-institution holdings remain in small, distributed archives whose contributions are difficult to integrate without loss of community or curatorial intent. This short paper presents a lightweight knowledge graph pattern: local, maintainable hubs that reuse external LOD (e.g. Wikidata) where appropriate while preserving bottom-up extension and interconnection suited to participatory or hybrid collection practices. We connect this pattern to user- and community-informed requirements for knowledge graph information systems in library contexts and to goals of FAIR and CARE-aware metadata—emphasizing interoperability without forcing a single authority structure. The aim is not a new ontology but a pragmatic architectural and governance sketch: how small graphs can remain trustworthy, attributable, and open to participation. We discuss trade-offs (curation vs. crowdsourcing, stability vs. agility) and position the approach within DCMI topics on emerging technologies, cultural and social dimensions of metadata, and metadata supporting reuse.

**Keywords (draft):** knowledge graphs; linked open data; participatory archives; digital libraries; interoperability; FAIR/CARE

## Angle 3 — Interaction patterns for polyvocal metadata in human–AI workflows

**Title (working):** Polyvocal knowledge acquisition: interaction patterns for metadata that align AI assistance with human values

**Abstract**

As AI is increasingly used to generate, suggest, or reconcile metadata, systems risk collapsing plural meaning into single automated labels unless human and community voices remain structurally visible in the workflow. This short paper proposes a set of interaction patterns for polyvocal knowledge acquisition—situations where experts, communities, and algorithms each contribute partial descriptions of resources. Patterns specify how provenance, approval, dissent, and alternative vocabularies are recorded so that downstream agents and interfaces can remain context-aware and accountable. We relate these patterns to DCMI’s theme of meaning-driven AI and to tracks on metadata and AI, AI agents, human-centered interaction, and ethics (bias, transparency). The contribution is conceptual and design-led, illustrated with a concise scenario from cultural or research information environments; it complements technical stacks by foregrounding metadata commitments needed so “assistance” does not override values enshrined in participation and care for marginalized narratives.

**Keywords (draft):** polyvocality; human-in-the-loop; metadata; AI; provenance; participatory design; ethics
