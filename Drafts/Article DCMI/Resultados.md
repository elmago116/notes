## HerStory (2024–27) – 200-word Summary

Date: 2025-08-11

HerStory&NeSyAI is a three-year research project (PID2023-147673OB-I00) that connects women’s history with Neuro‑Symbolic AI (NeSyAI) through an Information Architecture (IA) lens. Its goal is to mitigate gender bias in information systems by improving how data about women and marginalized genders is searched, navigated, organized, labeled, visualized, and narrated. The project advances IA theory and practice by integrating semantic web standards (RDF, linked open data) with neural models, enabling explainable narrative generation grounded in knowledge graphs and community co-created data.

The framework is structured in five layers: Conceptualization (Layer A), Design (Layer B), Development (Layer C), Implementation and Use in a case study (Layer D), and Co‑created Citizen Science (Layer E). Three research objectives guide the work: O1 updates IA with a gender‑aware NeSyAI perspective and ethical/legal guidelines; O2 designs gender‑inclusive NeSyAI prototypes for IA services via UCD, data transformation to LOD, neural model specification, training on the KG, symbolic‑neural reasoning, and UX/ethics evaluation; O3 applies and assesses the framework on the Francoist repression and censorship case study with a gender lens.

Methodologically, the project combines qualitative (co‑creation, focus groups, interviews, observation) and quantitative (log analytics, data mining, visualization) techniques within action research and “citizen digital humanities,” engaging Wikipedia/Wikidata activists and domain communities. This enables enriched cognitive and emotional readings, improves data dissemination and use, and fosters inclusive knowledge production.

Source: [UB Wikiwomen – HerStory (2024–27)](https://www.ub.edu/wikiwomen/research/herstory-2024-27/)

# Methodology
The analysis reveals that while co-occurrence volume increased, the inclusion algorithm prioritizes stronger relationships, resulting in more focused but conceptually robust keyword clusters.


This study consists of a scoping review based on SALSA (Search, Appraisal, Synthesis, Analysis) framework combined with a bibliometric technique called scientific maps (Cobo et al., 2011) to map the conceptual structure of the interdisciplinary field formed between the search interests. The SALSA framework guided the systematic exploration of academic literature through four sequential phases. (1) The Search phase utilized Perplexity AI for assistance (Codina, 2023) with search equations and refining results (Lopezosa et al., 2023) to get a significant sample of literature, both the actual documents and the most complete metadata that the search could gather to apply the bibliometric techniques effectively. This systematic data extraction from Scopus (2,657 records) and Web of Science (613 records) gathered from 2014 to 2025, yielding 3,270 unique records after deduplication. (2) The appraisal phase involved systematic evaluation and metadata corpus refinement, while the (3) synthesis step examined both general field structure and project-specific thematic positioning. For the bibliometric analysis, scientific mapping techniques (Snyder, 2019) were employed using SciMAT software (Cobo et al, 2012), co-word analysis to examine keyword co-occurrence networks (Callon et al., 1991) with a curated sample of 45,650 normalized keywords extracted from the academic records. The database’s search equations used focused, proximity, and broad operators such as `W/n`, `NEAR/n`, wild cards `*`, and field-specific labels to ensure both extension and theme presence without manually selecting the inclusion of metadata in the analysis and preventing bias. The analysis chose the simple centers clustering algorithm with inclusion index normalization (Cobo et al., 2011), generating a themed network with 14,151 nodes and 231,319 edges (Callon’s centrality and density measures) in two five-year periods (2014/2019 - P1 and 2020-2025- P2) to grasp what are called the strategic zones. Then the clusters formed with themed keywords are positioned into four quadrants: motor themes, specialized themes, emerging or declining themes, and basic and transversal themes (Cobo et al., 2018?) to describe the conceptual structure of the field in the last 10 years.  

 

The methodology enabled both quantitative mapping of the field’s conceptual structure and a qualitative identification of relevant projects and user-centered design or participatory design methodologies in GLAM (galleries, libraries, archives, and museums) and semantic web technologies and similars.
